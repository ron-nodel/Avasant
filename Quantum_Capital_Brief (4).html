Why the announcement view is so lumpy — and why 2026 looks near-zero there
Governments announce in political waves: first strategies (2018: US, Germany, Israel, Japan), stimulus (2020–21: Germany's €2B, France, Netherlands), the second strategy wave (2023: UK £2.5B + Korea + India in one year), then the sub-national wave (2025: Maryland, Illinois, New York, Spain). Between waves there is simply nothing new to announce. 2026 in that view is $93M for four reasons: the $2.0B CHIPS package is itemised to companies (section 1's green band — counted once, not twice); the year is only 58% done; the $2.7B NQI Reauthorization is proposed, not law; and nearly every major economy already announced in 2023–25. Governments announce in waves. Wave one: strategies (2018). Wave two: stimulus (2020–21). Wave three: larger strategies (2023). Wave four: regional programmes (2025). Between waves there is nothing new to announce. The 2026 bar in that view is $93M. The causes: (1) the $2.0B CHIPS money is counted in section 1, not here; (2) five months of the year remain; (3) the $2.7B US bill is not law; (4) most countries announced their strategies already.

3 · Venture vs public-market — two different signals

Venture (committed) Public-market (window)
Same money as figure 1, government grants removed, so only the two private-sector channels remain. Public-market = new shares issued by already-listed companies (IPO, SPAC, follow-on, at-the-market) — cash to the company, priced by the market, not by diligence.

Venture is diligenced, contractually committed capital — from ~$1B/yr (2021–24) to $4.3B (2025), $3.2B so far in 2026. Public-market money is window capital: it floods in when stocks run (2021, 2025–26) and vanishes when they don't (2023: $86M). Bull: public access is graduation. Bear: half the boom rides on a window that can close in a quarter. The one signal to watch — does blue keep growing if orange stops?

Venture money is committed. It grew from $1B each year (2021–24) to $4.3B (2025). Public-market money follows stock prices. In 2023 it almost stopped ($86M). Watch one signal: if the orange part stops, does the blue part continue to grow?

The bottom line

Two layers moving at different speeds. A small public/quasi-public elite — ten names, ~63% of capital, government equity stakes — whose funding tracks stock markets and policy. And a broad private base of 400+ companies whose venture funding is at record levels but four times smaller than the headline. The signal that decides which story is true: does venture keep climbing if the public window closes? The market has two layers. Layer one: ten large companies with approximately 63% of the money. Their money follows stock markets and government policy. Layer two: more than 400 private companies. Their venture money is at a record level, but it is four times smaller. Watch one signal: if the stock window closes, does venture money continue to increase?
